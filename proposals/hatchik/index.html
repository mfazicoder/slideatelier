Safe to experiment

Mess about freely.
The sandbox catches the mistakes.

Vibe-coding with an AI is fun precisely because you can move fast. But fast + your live customers' data = scary. Four ideas keep experiments safe — the first two are wired today, the others are on the near-term roadmap.

Rail 01
Live

The sandbox itself is a safe playground

Your free Sandbox is the private copy. It runs at yoursubdomain.hatchik.com with payments in test mode and no real customers, so you can break things freely. When you're ready for a real audience, you upgrade to Launch and we provision a separate production stack.

Rail 02
Live

Migrations don't run themselves

If your AI suggests a database change (adding a field, dropping one), it lands as a migration file in your repo — it does not auto-apply. You review the SQL, run it via Supabase Studio when you're happy, and CLAUDE.md tells your AI never to drop tables or columns without your say-so.

Rail 03
Roadmap

One-click rollback

Today: every change is a git commit, so any AI-coding tool can "go back to the previous version" by reverting. Automated nightly off-site backups with one-click restore are on the near-term roadmap; until they're live you can take an on-demand Postgres snapshot any time from Supabase Studio.

Rail 04
Roadmap

Read-only mode for risky changes

A toggle to put your live app in read-only mode while you experiment — customers can still browse, nothing they do can write — is on the roadmap for Launch tier. Until it ships, the workflow is: try the change in your Sandbox, gut-check it, then promote.

FAQ

Honest answers.

Do I really own my app and the code behind it?

Yes. The code repository is created under your GitHub account on day one. The domain is registered in your name. The server is rented to your account. If you cancel, you walk away with all of it. No obfuscation, no licensing tricks, no hostage situations.

What if I want to leave?

Cancel any time. We hand over the server keys, you keep the GitHub repo, the domain stays in your name, and your customers' data is yours. You can keep running everything yourself afterwards for roughly £10–15/month total (a small server, the domain renewal, transactional email). No exit fee, no data extraction charges.

What licence is my code under?

Your product code (anything you build inside the */product/ folders and your own database migrations) is yours, full stop — licence it however you want. The substrate code Hatchik provides is released under Business Source License 1.1. Plain English: you can use it for your own product anywhere forever, modify it, fork it, share it with your team, hire developers, sell to your own customers — all fine. The only thing you can't do is offer it to third parties as a competing "Hatchik-like service". After three years the substrate converts to Apache 2.0 automatically — fully open, no strings, no exceptions. The full text lives in the LICENSE file inside your sandbox repo.

What if I'm not a developer?

Hatchik is built for you. The product owners we have in mind are lawyers, restaurateurs, consultants, designers — people with a clear app idea but no infrastructure background. You'll drive an AI helper (Claude, Cursor or similar) to build the actual features, and we make sure that experience is safe — your AI can't accidentally break things, and you stay in charge of what launches. If you've never used an AI coding tool before, we walk you through it after signup.

How does paying actually work?

£89 one-time when you launch (covers your domain, hosting account, mailboxes, and full configuration — done in minutes). Then £14/month while you're getting your first users. After your 15th sign-up the monthly graduates to £39 — because by then you've got traction. You'll get an email a month before the change. The free Sandbox stays free forever; you only start paying when you want a real domain.

What currency will I pay in, and is tax included?

Prices on this page are shown in GBP for reference, but checkout auto-detects your country and shows you the equivalent in your local currency — USD, EUR, AUD, INR, BRL, AED and 25+ others. VAT (UK / EU), GST (Australia / India / Singapore), US state sales tax, and other local taxes are calculated and added automatically at checkout, with a proper tax invoice emailed to you. Customers in lower-income regions get an automatic purchasing-power discount applied. You never have to work out what the right amount is — your local price, with your local tax, in your local currency.

Why does my statement say "Paddle.com Market Ltd · Hatchik"?

Hatchik uses Paddle as our Merchant of Record — a well-trodden model used by many global SaaS products. Paddle is the legal seller, which means they handle all the boring tax compliance globally so we don't pass that cost (or complexity) on to you. The practical effect: your bank statement will read "Paddle.com Market Ltd · Hatchik". Your subscription, login, app, and support all stay with Hatchik — only the billing entity differs. Refunds run through Paddle and usually land within 5–10 days.

What if I'm not charging customers?

Hatchik works equally well whether you're selling to paying customers, building an internal tool for your team, running a community project, or just making the thing you've always wanted to exist. Stripe payments are wired in but optional — flip them on if you ever want to charge, leave them off if you don't. The Stage 2 graduation at 15 sign-ups is based on real people using your app, not on revenue, so the pricing works the same way whether you're monetising or not.

Will you submit my app to the App Store and Google Play?

No — and this one is non-negotiable. We build the iOS and Android shells from your code, ready to submit. You handle the submission itself, because the apps live on your store accounts under your legal identity. That means you'll need an Apple Developer Program account (~£99/year) and a Google Play Console account (~£25 one-time), and you're responsible for the listing metadata (description, screenshots, keywords, age rating), compliance with each store's review guidelines, privacy declarations (App Tracking Transparency, the Data Safety form), staged rollouts, and responding to review feedback. We're happy to advise on common rejection reasons and what each store looks for, but we don't press submit on your behalf. See §5.3 of our product spec for the full list.

How do backups actually work?

Honest answer: automated nightly off-site backups aren't wired yet — they're on the near-term roadmap and will be rolled out on Launch + Growth tiers. Today, you can take an on-demand Postgres snapshot any time from Supabase Studio (a couple of clicks) and download it locally — handy before any risky migration. When automated backups land we'll email everyone with the details; the plan is nightly with 14-day retention on Launch and 30-day retention on Growth, encrypted at rest and stored off-site in the same region as your server. Until then, if you'd like us to run a snapshot for you, email hello@hatchik.com and we'll do it the same day.

Payment will always bounce to your browser, because we'll never take card details through chat. Same outcome either way: your app on your domain, your code in your GitHub.

How quickly do I actually get my Hatchik after signup?

Free Sandbox: ~60–90 seconds from form submit to a working subdomain at yoursubdomain.hatchik.com, with the sign-in email landing right after. Launch tier (your own domain, mailboxes, server in your region) takes a bit longer because we register the domain and wire DNS — that path is currently a hand-onboarded step, so expect an email from the founder within the hour and your stack live within 24 hours while we automate the last mile. Same product either way; only the provisioning path differs.

Is it safe to let my AI manage my live app?

Today the boundary is your free Sandbox: your AI builds and pushes freely there, breaks nothing real (test-mode payments, no live customers), and you only promote to a Launch tier with a real domain once you're happy. Inside the substrate we already gate destructive actions — CLAUDE.md tells your AI never to drop tables or columns without your say-so, migrations land as files for you to apply, and your repo's commit history makes any change reversible. Explicit browser confirmations for money-moving or data-destructive AI actions are on the roadmap once we have customers running real payments; for now, "use the Sandbox first" is the rail.

When you upgrade to Launch, you can ask for Finland, US East, US West or Singapore and we'll provision a host there once we have demand — all Hetzner, same hardware family. Your server is provisioned in your name. If you have specific rules to meet (GDPR for EU customers, HIPAA for US healthcare), tell us at upgrade and we'll match the region; we'll also send you the relevant paperwork.

What if my app needs AI features inside it?

Many apps in 2026 use AI to generate text, summarise content, chat with users, or recommend things at runtime — that's different from the AI that helps you write the code (your Cursor or Claude subscription handles that). Hatchik pre-wires integrations with Claude, GPT, Gemini and open-source models, and gives you two ways to pay for the usage: bring your own provider key (you get billed by the provider directly, no Hatchik involvement), or route through us for one bill, an included token allowance, and built-in cost caps. Either way, switching between providers is a config change, not a rewrite. If your app doesn't use AI internally, ignore it — it's optional.

Can I use my own AI API key instead of paying Hatchik?

Yes. By default that's exactly how it works — you put your Anthropic, OpenAI, or OpenRouter key into your app's .env file and the provider bills you directly. No Hatchik involvement, no margin on top, no lock-in. Hatchik's optional passthrough (one bill, included token allowance, vendor switching without code changes, cost caps) is a convenience for customers who want unified billing — it's not a requirement. You can switch between BYO and passthrough at any time, and the substrate code reads from environment variables that work with either path.

How does the to-do list work?

When we provision your sandbox we drop a BACKLOG.md file at the root of your repo pre-populated with about twenty starter tasks based on what you described. As your AI assistant works on features, it ticks them off — it reads and writes that file like any other. You see progress at a glance and can re-order, add new ideas, or leave notes inline. BACKLOG.md works with any tool you point at the repo — and if you'd rather use a proper tracker, just wire your own in. We don't make that decision for you.

Do you build my actual product?

No — and that's deliberate. We give you the foundation: hosting, domain, payments, mail, mobile builds, security. The actual product (the features your customers pay for) you build with your AI helper, or by hand, or by hiring someone. We're the boring infrastructure underneath, not the architect. If you want an AI tool to build your whole app from a prompt, look at Lovable or Bolt — they're great for prototypes but tend to hit a wall when you need real business plumbing. Hatchik is what's underneath after that.

What if I outgrow Hatchik?

A single dedicated VPS will comfortably carry a Launch-tier app well into its first thousand paying users. When you need more, the underlying setup is plain Docker + Postgres + Caddy — moving to a bigger server (or splitting across several) is hours of work, not weeks. At that point you've probably hired your first developer; we'll help them with the handover.
