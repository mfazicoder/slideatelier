# Hatchik — Pre-launch Comms Drafts

Drafts for day-1 announcements (warm circles only). All copy is honest about
the waitlist: we're not live yet, and we'll only email when we are.

Tone notes (consistent across all channels):
- British English, plain language first.
- Lead with the pain, not the features.
- No fake scarcity ("limited spots!"). No fake urgency ("launching soon!").
- Specifics over abstractions.
- The reader is a busy founder / professional with an idea, not a developer.

---

## 1. Personal-network email

For sending to 20-50 close contacts — friends, ex-colleagues, anyone you'd
have grabbed a coffee with about the idea anyway. Send **individually** (or
as a small BCC if you must), not as a campaign blast. Personal subject line.
A reply is more valuable than a signup.

> **Subject**: a small thing I'd love your honest take on
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> Hope you're well. Brief context: I've been quietly building something for
> the last [N] months and I just put up a landing page for it. Before I start
> properly announcing it I'd love your honest take.
>
> The idea: most people with a genuine app idea — a restaurateur tired of
> clipboards, a wedding planner drowning in spreadsheets, a consultant who
> wants to ship a real tool not a deck — give up because the boring bit
> (hosting, payments, mail, mobile, security) eats three months before
> anything reaches a customer. AI coders like Claude and Cursor are great at
> writing features, but they're not great at standing up a real production
> stack with a domain and a database and an iOS shell. I'm building the
> production stack so they don't have to.
>
> Landing page: <https://hatchik.com>
>
> It's a waitlist for now — the wizard isn't live yet, we're a few weeks out.
> I'd love either:
>
> 1. Honest reactions — does the page make sense? Where do you stall? What
>    feels suspicious or wrong?
> 2. People you know who might want this — feel free to forward.
>
> No pressure either way. Genuinely just want a sanity check from people I
> trust before this goes wider.
>
> Cheers,
> [Your name]

## 6. Reply templates (for incoming signups & DMs)

When someone replies to your personal email or DMs after a tweet, don't let
the conversation die. Templates to copy-paste:

### When someone says "interesting, signed up":

> Thanks for jumping on the list. Quick question while I have you — what
> are you currently building (or wanting to build)? Trying to make sure
> Hatchik is shaped right for the people who'll actually use it.

### When someone says "this looks like [competitor]":

> Fair callout. Two main differences: (1) the substrate lives on the
> customer's own infrastructure, not ours — including the GitHub repo, the
> domain, the server keys; and (2) AI-coder integration is first-class, not
> bolted on. Happy to dig into specifics — what specifically did you
> think of?

### When someone asks "when does it actually launch":

> We're a few weeks out. The current honest answer: substrate template is
> nearly done, the sign-up wizard is about half done, the orchestrator (the
> bit that actually provisions your server in 5 minutes) is the last
> milestone. I'll email everyone on the list the moment it's live — no
> drip campaign in between.

### When someone wants early access:

> Genuinely tempting offer to take you up on. The honest answer is that the
> orchestrator isn't ready yet, so I'd be doing the provisioning by hand —
> which I'm happy to do for the first 5 people IF you're willing to be a
> patient guinea pig. Reply with what you'd be building and I'll see if it's
> a fit for the early cohort.

## 7. What NOT to say

- ❌ "Limited spots available!" (waitlists aren't limited)
- ❌ "Pre-order now!" (not selling anything yet)
- ❌ "Revolutionary AI-powered platform" (delete the word "revolutionary")
- ❌ "10x faster than [competitor]" (you don't have data)
- ❌ "Trusted by [companies]" (you have no customers yet)
- ❌ Any reference to "users" or "customers" you don't actually have
- ❌ "Coming soon™" with a TM symbol
- ❌ "Disrupting [industry]"
